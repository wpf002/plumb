Scenario 2: "What happens to valuation if the business can operate without me?"
  Counterintuitive finding from this dataset: Steve's management-role pay (the opex-allocated, burdened
  portion of his comp, $122,773/yr, ~$101,465/yr unburdened) is
  actually BELOW the ~$150,000/yr it would cost to hire a market-rate GM/estimator/PM to replace
  him (a gap of ~$48,535/yr). So reported EBITDA does NOT overstate earnings via
  excess owner comp the way it does for many owner-operator sellers - but it also means bringing in outside
  management would compress EBITDA by roughly that gap, not expand it. The real lever for a higher multiple is
  not a comp addback - it's proving the business can retain the Musco relationship, estimating accuracy, and
  crew leadership without Steve personally in the room. If Steve spends the next 2-3 years developing a #2
  (e.g., foreman Danny Ruiz) into an estimating/PM/relationship role and documents that transition to Musco,
  buyers would likely move from the LOW end of the multiple range toward the HIGH end or above it (illustratively
  3.5-4.5x normalized EBITDA instead of 3.0-4.2x), because key-person risk - the single largest valuation
  discount factor identified in this dataset - would be reduced.
  Illustrative re-rate: normalized EBITDA $564,805 x 3.5-4.5x =
  $1,976,818 - $2,541,624 Enterprise Value (vs. $1,694,416-$2,372,182 today).

Scenario 3: "What happens if Musco-related revenue falls 25%?"
  2025 Musco revenue was approximately $1,508,083 (54.1% of total).
  A 25% decline removes approximately $377,021 of revenue.
  Musco projects carry a materials-heavy, labor-light cost structure (~20-25% direct labor vs. ~28-32% on
  other project types), so the GROSS MARGIN impact of losing Musco work is worse than losing an equivalent
  dollar of other commercial work - fixed overhead (admin payroll, shop costs, depreciation, Steve's
  management comp) does not shrink proportionally. Estimated EBITDA impact: a 25% Musco revenue decline
  (~$377,021 revenue loss at ~30% contribution margin after
  variable costs) flows through to roughly a $113,106 reduction
  in EBITDA, which alone would reduce normalized EBITDA by more than 20% - directly compressing the valuation
  range in Scenario 1 by a similar proportion. This scenario is the clearest illustration of why customer
  concentration is flagged as a material valuation risk.
