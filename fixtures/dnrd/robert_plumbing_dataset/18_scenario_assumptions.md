Scenario 2: "What happens if I exit Residential Service entirely and focus only on Install + Commercial?"
  Residential Service generated $1,630,630 in 2025 (down from $1,977,521
  in 2022, a segment already in structural decline). At an estimated ~30% contribution
  margin after direct labor and materials, exiting it removes roughly $489,189
  of annual contribution - a real EBITDA hit unless the ~14 residential-service techs/dispatchers can be redeployed into
  growing Residential Install work (which needs different skills/licensing emphasis but overlaps substantially for
  plumbers and HVAC techs). It also eliminates emergency/service-call relationships that are the top of the funnel for
  new install and maintenance-agreement customers - most new maintenance-agreement signups historically originate from
  a prior service call. Pure exit without a redeployment plan is value-destructive; a *managed* wind-down that converts
  residential service capacity into install capacity and agreement-conversion is more defensible, but is a 12-18 month
  transition, not a switch flip.

Scenario 4: "What would make the company more valuable to a buyer?"
  Computed from this dataset, the specific, prioritized levers are:
  1. Reverse the EBITDA margin decline (14.7% in 2022 to 13.7% in 2025) - a buyer will
     ask why margin compressed while revenue grew, and "we don't know" is a bigger discount than almost any single
     operational fix. This dataset's compression is traceable to COGS growing faster than revenue in 2023-2024 before
     a partial 2025 recovery - that story needs a clear, data-backed explanation (wage inflation, project mix, or
     estimating discipline) before a sale process starts.
  2. Grow recurring maintenance-agreement revenue further: it rose from 6.5% of revenue in 2022 to
     14.7% in 2025 - continuing that trend toward 20%+ materially re-rates the multiple, since recurring,
     contracted revenue is valued higher than one-time service/install work.
  3. Reduce customer concentration: top-5 tracked (maintenance + install) customers are 22.1% of that revenue base,
     with the largest single customer at 5.5% - not alarming today, but worth documenting as a strength rather
     than leaving a buyer to discover and discount for it.
  4. De-risk key-person dependence: formalize the GM's authority over day-to-day operations and estimating sign-off
     before a sale process begins, and document a transition plan for Robert's commercial-account relationships -
     today's data shows a GM in place since 2016, which is a real asset, but it has not been stress-tested with
     Robert fully absent.
  5. Real estate: the company owns its 14,500-sqft shop/warehouse with $1,472,820 of net
     equity ($1,760,000 market value less $287,180 mortgage) - deciding
     whether to sell it with the business, lease it back, or retain it personally is a distinct decision that materially
     changes headline sale price without changing operating-business value, and should be decided deliberately rather
     than by default.
