
JOE — "SHOULD I ADD A SECOND PROJECT CREW?" (computed from actual dataset)

DEMAND SIGNAL (already in the data):
  - Existing project crew utilization: 87% average over the
    trailing 24 months, peaking near 94% in 2024 (see 20d_project_crew_utilization.csv).
  - Lost opportunities (bids declined solely because the crew was fully booked) totaled approximately
    $373,800 in 2024 and $320,600 in 2025 (see 15c_lost_opportunities.csv) - averaging
    $347,200/year in identifiable turned-away demand, which alone would be enough to fill
    most of a second crew's early-year capacity.

COST OF A SECOND CREW:
  - New hires (crew lead + 2 installers + 1 equipment operator + 1 laborer): $241,280/yr in wages,
    $299,187/yr fully burdened (payroll tax, workers comp, benefits).
  - Equipment: used truck ($42,000) + trailer ($7,500) +
    mini excavator ($48,000) + skid steer ($34,000)
    + hand tools ($9,000) = $140,500 total.
  - Financing: 15% down ($21,075 cash), remaining $119,425 financed
    over 60 months at 7.9% -> $2,416/month ($28,990/yr debt service).
  - Incremental insurance: ~$9,600/yr. Incremental fuel/maintenance: ~$14,000/yr.
  - NOTE: new crew labor is a direct cost already reflected in the gross-margin assumption below, not
    double-counted as a separate fixed cost.

REVENUE RAMP (a brand-new crew does not launch at full utilization -- scheduling, reputation, and training take time):
  - Year 1 (~45% of a mature crew's capacity): ~$621,000 revenue
  - Year 2 (~60%): ~$828,000 revenue
  - Year 3 (~72%, approaching the existing crew's 2024 peak): ~$993,600 revenue
  - Applying the business's actual historical average project gross margin (33.7%):
      Year 1 net contribution after new fixed costs (debt service + incremental insurance/fuel): $156,772
      Year 2 net contribution: $226,560
      Year 3 net contribution: $282,390
  - Breakeven revenue needed just to cover the NEW fixed costs (debt service + insurance + fuel) at the
    historical margin: ~$155,989/yr -- about 11% of a
    mature crew's full annual capacity, comfortably below the Year-1 ramp estimate above.

CASH TIMING (the real risk, not profitability):
  - Cash on hand as of Dec-2025: $261,105. The $21,075 down payment plus 2-3 months of
    new payroll running ahead of new-crew billings (new crews take 60-90 days to build a full project
    pipeline) could draw working cash down by roughly $83,406
    in the first two to three months before new project billings catch up -- manageable against current cash,
    but worth sequencing (e.g., hire in Q1 when maintenance-crew cash flow is seasonally building, not Q4).

BOTTOM LINE:
  The data supports adding a second crew: demonstrated turned-away demand (~$347,200/yr) exceeds
  the revenue needed to profitably cover the new fixed costs, and the existing crew's margin profile, applied
  to even a conservative first-year ramp, produces positive net contribution in Year 1. The real risk is NOT
  long-run profitability -- it's short-term cash timing during the ramp-up and whether Joe can actually convert
  turned-away leads into booked, scheduled work fast enough to hit the Year-1 revenue assumption. This is NOT
  a slam-dunk "yes" -- it depends on utilization discipline in year one, which is exactly the kind of judgment
  call DNRD should help Joe monitor against plan, not something a static ROI calculation alone resolves.
